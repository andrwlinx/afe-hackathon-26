## Slide 1 — The Problem

# "Who do I even ask?"

**It's day 3 of your internship. Something in the pipeline broke.**

- Ownership is scattered across Bindles, Pipelines, wikis, and tribal knowledge
- New interns don't know who owns anything — or who's safe to ask
- Asking the wrong person wastes everyone's time; asking nobody wastes yours

*Every new engineer loses days to this. Interns lose weeks.*

> Speaker notes: Open with the day-3 intern story. The pain isn't missing
> documentation — it's that ownership knowledge lives in six disconnected
> systems and in senior engineers' heads.

## Slide 2 — RampPath

# Ask in plain English. Get a verified answer.

**"Who owns AtlasRegionContext?"**

- **Knowledge graph** of people, teams, packages, pipelines, bindles, and AWS accounts
- **Ranked people with explainable scores** — keyword relevance × relationship strength × recency, with the math shown
- **Evidence-backed** — every answer traces a verified path through source records; it never invents facts
- **Lives inside Phone Tool** — a native A-to-Z tab, one click from the profile page, with contact links back to Phone Tool

*(Full-bleed product screenshot here: the graph + ranked contacts view)*

> Speaker notes: Emphasize the two differentiators — explainability (the
> "why this person" breakdown) and evidence (source records, no
> hallucination). Fuzzy plain-text queries work too: "who owns metrics?"
> offers clickable options.
